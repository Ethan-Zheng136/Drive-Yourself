\begin{abstract}
End-to-end driving policies are trained to reproduce a single consensus behavior
over large trajectory corpora, and the resulting planner behaves as one average
driver: it drives the same way for every user and every scene. The gap this
creates is not only about personalization. On the same scenarios, planners with
comparable closed-loop safety can occupy very different regions of a
multi-dimensional behavioral profile, so optimizing safety alone leaves driving
style unconstrained and largely unmeasured; and when several maneuvers are
equally reasonable, a single-mode decoder tends to interpolate between them
rather than commit to one.

We study driving style as a controllable quantity and present YouDrive, a
personalized end-to-end framework built on a vision-language-action (VLA)
backbone. YouDrive has three parts. A style metric, YDMS, first gates hard safety
and task requirements and then scores behavior along interpretable,
scenario-normalized style dimensions, so that style is assessed on its own terms
rather than folded into a single safety number. A style axis represents each
persona as a task vector, a low-rank weight edit distilled from a teacher
planner, and exposes it in the VLA content space through one interpolation
coefficient that scales and composes personas without retraining the backbone. A
flow-matching decoder replaces the discrete action codebook of the backbone,
samples several dynamically feasible candidates, and returns one, so that
multi-modal scenes are resolved by commitment instead of averaging.

On NAVSIM navtest, YouDrive covers a wide and controllable range of driving
styles while retaining strong closed-loop safety, reaching 0.954 PDMS, and we
report a set of studies that includes persona blends and an ensemble-decoder
variant of the same idea. We treat YDMS as a first step and analyze its current
limitations openly. Code and the metric will be released.
\end{abstract}
