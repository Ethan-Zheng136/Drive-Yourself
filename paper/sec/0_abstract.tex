\begin{abstract}
End-to-end driving policies imitate a consensus of human trajectories, and the
resulting planner behaves as a single average driver with no user-specific style
control. This is not only a personalization gap: on identical scenarios, planners
with comparable closed-loop safety occupy very different regions of a
multi-dimensional behavioral profile, so optimizing safety alone leaves driving
style unconstrained and largely unmeasured, and regression-style decoding tends to
average equally plausible maneuvers rather than commit to one.

We present YouDrive, a personalized end-to-end planner built on a
vision-language-action (VLA) backbone. At its core, a flow-matching decoder
replaces the backbone's discrete action codebook: it samples several dynamically
feasible trajectories in a kinematic control space and commits to one, resolving
equally plausible maneuvers by selection rather than by a non-committal,
between-mode average that current safety scores do not penalize. On top of this
decoder we expose driving style as a control axis: each persona is a low-rank
weight-space edit\,---\,a task vector in the task-arithmetic sense\,---\,that we
realize at inference as a direction in the VLA content space, so a single
coefficient scales and composes personas without retraining the backbone. To
measure style beyond a single safety number, YDMS gates hard safety and task
requirements and then scores behavior along interpretable, scenario-normalized
style dimensions.

On NAVSIM navtest, driver identity explains far more of the behavioral variance
than coarse style labels (mean $\eta^2 \approx 0.27$ vs.\ $\le 0.09$), and a
single coefficient traces a monotone path across the style axes while closed-loop
safety is retained, with the strongest configuration reaching 0.954 PDMS. We
analyze where YDMS is discriminative and where it remains weak. Code and the
metric will be released.
\end{abstract}
