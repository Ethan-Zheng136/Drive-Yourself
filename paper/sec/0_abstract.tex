\begin{abstract}
End-to-end driving policies imitate a consensus of human trajectories and behave
as a single average driver with no user-specific style control. Beyond
personalization, planners with comparable safety occupy very different regions of
a behavioral profile: optimizing safety alone leaves style unconstrained and
unmeasured, and single-mode decoding averages equally plausible maneuvers instead
of committing to one. We present YouDrive, a personalized planner on a
vision-language-action (VLA) backbone. Its core is a flow-matching decoder that
replaces the discrete action codebook, samples several dynamically feasible
trajectories in a kinematic control space, and commits to one. On top of it, style
becomes a control axis: each persona is a low-rank weight edit\,---\,a task
vector\,---\,realized as a direction in the VLA content space, so one coefficient
scales and composes personas without retraining the backbone. To measure style
rather than only safety, we introduce YDMS, a measurement framework that gates
hard safety and task requirements, then scores interpretable, scenario-normalized
style dimensions. On NAVSIM navtest, YDMS separates structure that safety scores
and coarse categories collapse: driver identity explains far more behavioral
variance than such categories (mean $\eta^2 \approx 0.27$ vs.\ $\le 0.09$). A
single coefficient traces a monotone, controllable path while closed-loop safety
stays above 0.90 PDMS throughout (up to 0.954). Code and the metric will be
released.
\end{abstract}
