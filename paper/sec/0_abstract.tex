\begin{abstract}
End-to-end autonomous driving is trained on large trajectory corpora under
objectives that favor a single consensus behavior, yielding a policy that acts
as one \emph{average driver}: the same deterministic style for every user and
scene. We argue that driving style---a measurable, interpretable aspect of
\emph{how} motion is executed---is the dimension this paradigm erases, and that
the widely reported drop in closed-loop scores when style is emphasized does not
imply an inevitable style--safety trade-off, especially when safety metrics do
not distinguish behaviors that users treat as different.
We present \textbf{YouDrive}, which (i) measures driving style on shared
benchmarks with a human-anchored, multi-planner style map, (ii) exposes style as
an explicit, user-steerable axis via latent content interpolation over
persona vectors on a vision--language--action backbone, and (iii) generates
trajectories with a style-conditioned flow-matching planner so that
multi-modal scenes admit decisive, sample-based choices rather than
token-level averaging. On NAVSIM, YouDrive spans a wide, controllable style
range while retaining strong closed-loop safety (PDMS up to

map will be released.
\end{abstract}
