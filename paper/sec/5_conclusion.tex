\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied driving style as a controllable quantity and presented YouDrive, which
separates how a vehicle drives from whether it drives safely. YouDrive measures style
with YDMS, a metric that gates safety and task requirements before scoring
interpretable style dimensions; represents each persona as a task vector in the
content space of a VLA backbone, controlled by one interpolation coefficient; and
decodes trajectories with a flow-matching head that samples several feasible
candidates and returns one. On NAVSIM, style moves over a wide range while closed-loop
safety stays high, reaching 0.954 PDMS, and a variance analysis shows that the style
we control is a systematic per-driver signal that coarse labels largely miss.

The current work has clear limits. YDMS is a first version: some of its dimensions
separate weakly on navtest, and validating it against human preference is left for
future work. The extended v2 results and several ablations are still being filled in.
Enriching the style axis, and closing these experimental gaps, are the immediate next
steps.
